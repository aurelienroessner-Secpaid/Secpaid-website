Stripe or PayPal closed my account: what to do next

If Stripe or PayPal closed your account, three problems need attention this week: the money still sitting in the account, your customers’ stored card data, and a new provider that will take your business. Each provider’s own terms set the dates that matter.

Start with this order of work:

  • Save the closure notice and every email around it. It names the reason category and the appeal route.
  • Do not close or delete the account yourself. Stripe says dashboard and API access continue as long as you do not close or delete it, and you may need that access to export data.
  • Ask the provider in writing for the reason, the amount held and the release date.
  • Start your application with SecPaid today, so you can keep taking payments and protect business continuity while underwriting runs. SecPaid takes care of the heavy lifting, including the card data transfer, so you can focus on your business.
  • Tell your finance lead the payout timeline in the next section, so cash flow planning starts now.

This is information, not legal advice. Contract terms differ by country and by account, so check your own agreement.

What the provider can do under its terms

All three providers reserve the right to end the contract, and each gives itself room to act fast when it sees a restricted activity.

ProviderEnding the contractHolding your money
StripeMay terminate or close the account “at any time”. A 10-day cure period applies to breaches that can be cured.May deduct from a reserve, from funds payable or from the balance.
PayPal (Germany)Two months’ notice for termination at its convenience. Termination for good cause at any time.Funds can sit in a reserve account, for more than 180 days where the restricted activity gives PayPal that right.
AdyenStandard notice is two months. Immediate termination is possible for suspected breach or when a scheme or acquirer demands it.The deposit is released in monthly increments, with a standard timeframe of six months.

The entries for Stripe and Adyen come from summaries of the terms pages, so read the current text of your own contract. Payment services law also sets a floor. Article 55 of PSD2 lets a provider end an open-ended contract with at least two months’ notice when the contract allows it. Whether a clause that allows termination “at any time” holds up for business users in the EEA is a legal question, and our research found no authority on it. Ask a lawyer.

Your money: how long payouts stay held

Plan for at least 120 days of restricted payouts with Stripe and more than 180 days with PayPal in the worst case.

Stripe’s Unacceptable Risk Policy for Germany says Stripe will typically restrict or pause payouts for 120 days from the date of its initial email. It gives the purpose as reanalysis and covering refunds, disputes and chargebacks. After that, a formal review can release all or part of the balance, keep holding it, or cancel the payout if the risk persists or the law requires it. The notice states the reason category, the actions applied, deadlines and how to appeal. Appeals go through a manual review.

PayPal’s German user agreement says PayPal may hold funds in the reserve account for more than 180 days where its rights arise from a restricted activity. Use the Resolution Center to ask for information on a limitation, hold or reserve.

You may read that providers keep money for 90 to 180 days or up to two years. Those figures come from anecdotes, not from Stripe’s terms. Use the dates in your own notice.

Work out your refund and chargeback exposure over the same period. The provider will net those against your balance before it releases anything.

Your customers: card data and recurring billing

Stripe will move stored card data to another processor, but only a PCI DSS Level 1 processor, and it does not move everything.

Stripe’s data migration page says customers own the sensitive data they entrust to Stripe, and Stripe will work with your new processor to transfer it. The new processor must provide its current attestation of compliance (or a Visa Global Registry listing) and a PGP public key of at least 4096 bits. The export is an encrypted file with card details, email addresses and metadata. It excludes payment history, subscriptions and credentials saved through Stripe Link.

The page gives no timeline and does not say whether the export is available once Stripe has closed or restricted an account. That is the first question to put to Stripe support.

Recurring billing needs extra care. Stripe’s import guide advises building the integration first, sending new customers to the new provider while existing customers stay on the old one, and labelling stored cards correctly as off-session. Card changes made at the old provider between export and import are lost. When the move is complete, confirm the old provider has cancelled every automatic billing schedule so customers are not charged twice.

Stripe says imports typically finish within 10 business days of receiving correct data, and that the previous processor may take a few days or several weeks to send it.

The MATCH list and why it matters

MATCH is Mastercard’s database of terminated merchants, and a listing follows you to every new provider that checks it.

Stripe’s MATCH guide explains that acquirers must screen applicants against MATCH and report terminated merchants. Listings last five years. Only the acquirer that made a listing can request its removal or correction, and removal is limited to entries made in error or, for PCI non-compliance, once compliance is verified. Acquirers must add a qualifying merchant within one business day of termination.

The list is not a public search tool. Stripe does not disclose MATCH information to users, so you ask your former acquirer or a prospective processor. Stripe says a MATCH or Visa VMSS listing generally disqualifies a merchant from processing with it.

What gets a merchant listed is a reason code. Examples include excessive chargebacks, excessive fraud, illegal transactions and violation of standards. No source we found says how often a closure for category reasons leads to a listing. A closure without fraud or chargeback grounds should not by itself meet the quantitative codes, but ask the provider which code, if any, applies to you. Get the answer in writing.

Choosing and onboarding a new provider

Onboarding speed depends heavily on how complete your documents are.

A German provider such as PAYONE asks for a company self-disclosure, a commercial register excerpt (Handelsregisterauszug) not older than six months, ID copies of the authorised signatories, a bank confirmation with IBAN and company name for the payout account, and proof of PCI compliance. Its risk check also reads your terms and conditions (AGB), legal notice (Impressum) and privacy notice, along with your product range and checkout. Supplement merchants should add product labels and ingredient lists, and should have the BVL notification to hand, because supplements must be notified to the BVL before their first sale in Germany. Ask each provider for its own checklist.

PAYONE says onboarding takes about nine business days when all documents arrive complete. That is one provider’s estimate, so ask others for theirs, and expect longer when documents are missing or your website contradicts your application.

When you compare providers, ask these questions:

  • Does the provider name your product category as permitted, in writing?
  • What reserve does it apply, and for how long?
  • Can it accept a card data transfer from your old provider?
  • Does it support recurring billing and the payment methods your customers use?
  • What is the notice period if it decides to end the contract?

Run the old and new providers side by side until the migration finishes, and start with a small share of traffic.

Frequently asked questions

How long will Stripe hold my money?

Stripe’s Unacceptable Risk Policy says it will typically restrict or pause payouts for 120 days from the date of its initial email, then run a formal review. Your notice states the specifics.

Can I still export my customers’ card data after Stripe closes my account?

Stripe’s documentation does not say. It says dashboard and API access continue as long as you do not close or delete the account, so avoid doing either, and ask Stripe support in writing.

Am I on the MATCH list?

You cannot search it yourself. Ask your former acquirer, or ask a prospective processor to check during underwriting.

Can I appeal a closure?

Stripe describes a manual review, and further appeals through the dashboard. PayPal points to its Resolution Center for information on holds and reserves. Neither guarantees a reversal.
