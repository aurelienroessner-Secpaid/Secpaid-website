Can I use Mollie, Adyen or Shopify Payments for CBD or supplements?

Adyen restricts CBD and supplements, Shopify Payments does not support CBD at all, and Mollie accepts supplements after extra checks on certification and ingredients. That is the short answer to whether you can use Mollie, Adyen or Shopify Payments for CBD or supplements, based on each provider’s published policy on 29 September 2026.

A published policy is only the first filter. Each provider’s underwriting team decides on your business, so “restricted” means “apply and be reviewed”, and “accepted” can still end in a decline. Policies also change, so open the linked pages before you rely on this table. Where a provider publishes a German version, we linked that one.

Mollie

Mollie says it accepts supplement businesses and asks for more information before it does.

Its supplements page reads: “As part of the onboarding process we will work with you to understand the product and supplements that you sell, as we may need more information on product certification and ingredients used.” The page also says chargebacks are more prevalent in supplements. It carries a November 2022 byline, so treat it as background, not current policy.

Mollie’s formal list of products it does not accept sits in a help article. That article was not readable when we checked, so this guide states no Mollie position on CBD. Open the article and ask Mollie support before you build on it.

Expect questions about certificates, ingredient lists and the claims on your product pages.

Adyen

Adyen lists CBD and supplements as restricted for merchants, which means approval with extra documents, and prohibited for platforms.

The Adyen list, last updated 13 November 2025, has these entries:

  • “Nutraceuticals and food products (highly regulated substances e.g., CBD, kava kava)”: restricted for merchants, prohibited for platform and partner businesses.
  • “Nutraceuticals and food products, excluding highly regulated substances”: restricted for merchants and platforms.
  • “Pharmaceuticals, prescription medicine and medical devices”: restricted.
  • “Cannabis (only in Canada)”: restricted.

Adyen says it will require additional documentation before onboarding restricted categories. Its terms also allow immediate termination when a merchant materially changes its product offering without written approval, according to a summary of the page. So describe your products accurately at application, and tell Adyen before you add a category.

Shopify Payments

Shopify Payments does not support CBD, hemp or THC products, and its position on ordinary supplements is unclear.

Shopify’s hemp page says: “Shopify Payments doesn’t support the sale of hemp, cannabidiol (CBD), and tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) products.” It adds that third-party providers may support these products, and each vets merchants itself.

The eligibility page lists cannabis and prescription drugs as prohibited. It restricts “pharmaceuticals and other products that make health claims that are not verified by a local or national regulatory body.” It does not name supplements, nutraceuticals or weight-loss products.

Because the page is silent, do not assume supplements are safe, and do not assume they are banned. Read Shopify’s acceptable use policy and payments terms, and expect scrutiny of your health claims. If you sell CBD on Shopify, you need a third-party gateway.

Stripe, PayPal, Klarna and Checkout.com

Stripe restricts CBD and screens supplements, PayPal is silent in the pages reviewed, and Klarna bans ingestible CBD across the EU.

Stripe (German version, dated 22 September 2026) prohibits “cannabis products” and “CBD products with THC levels greater than the applicable local jurisdiction’s legal limit”. It restricts “CBD products containing only negligible amounts of THC, per local limits”. It prohibits “pseudo-pharmaceuticals or nutraceuticals that are not safe or make harmful claims”, which leaves ordinary supplements to review.

PayPal’s German acceptable use policy needs approval for prescription products and for providing prescriptions, and restricts intoxicants and certain controlled substances. It does not mention CBD, hemp or supplements, so silence gives you no clearance.

Klarna states that “ingestible CBD products (e.g. oils, gummies, teas, supplements, capsules, vapes) are prohibited in all EU member states”. It permits topical and cosmetic CBD subject to market-specific requirements. This is a Klarna rule for Klarna as a payment method, and it applies even when Klarna is offered through Mollie.

Checkout.com’s regulated platform policy puts dietary supplements and nutraceuticals under pre-approval and lists CBD as restricted. That document covers payment facilitators, so a direct merchant may face different terms.

What to do if you are declined

A decline is a data point about that provider’s risk appetite, and it does not settle whether your business can be paid for.

  • Ask for the reason in writing, and whether it is a policy decision or a document gap.
  • Fix document gaps first: product certificates, ingredient lists, lab reports and evidence for every health claim on your site.
  • Check your regulatory status. For CBD, Germany’s BVL says CBD products are not marketable as food because no novel food authorisation exists. In September 2026 EFSA issued its first positive opinion on a synthetic CBD supplement, and the Commission and Member States still have to decide on authorisation.
  • Apply to providers that state your category in their policy, and avoid scattering applications across many providers, because each decline adds friction.
  • Keep your old integration running until the new provider is live.
A supplement or CBD business that describes itself accurately can be underwritten. One that misdescribes itself risks closure later. See our guide on what to do when Stripe or PayPal closes your account.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Mollie for CBD?

The Mollie pages reviewed do not state a CBD position, and its list of unaccepted products could not be read. Open the help article and ask Mollie support before you build a checkout on it.

Can I use Shopify Payments for CBD?

No. Shopify says Shopify Payments does not support hemp, CBD or THC products, and points to third-party providers.

Does Adyen accept supplements?

Adyen lists supplements as restricted for merchants, which means it reviews each business and asks for extra documents. CBD falls under a separate highly regulated entry, restricted for merchants and prohibited for platforms.

Is restricted the same as banned?

No. A restricted category can be approved after underwriting. A prohibited category will not be. If your provider has declined you or you want to compare options for CBD or supplements, talk to SecPaid.
