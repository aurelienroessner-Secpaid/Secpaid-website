Visa's VAMP Rules Explained for Pharmacies and Health Shops

If your payment provider has recently asked about your chargebacks, or sent you an email mentioning "VAMP", this is why. Visa tightened its rules in 2026, and providers are passing the pressure down to merchants.

Here's what it means for a small pharmacy or health webshop, without the technical noise.

What is VAMP?

VAMP stands for the Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program. It's how Visa keeps an eye on fraud and disputes (chargebacks) on online card payments.

It replaced Visa's older fraud and dispute programmes in 2025 with one single number: the VAMP ratio.

How the VAMP ratio works

In simple terms:

VAMP ratio = (fraud reports + disputes) ÷ your online Visa transactions, measured each month.

A few points that surprise people:

  • It counts the number of cases, not the money. A €10 dispute counts the same as a €500 one.
  • Fraud and disputes are added together. A fraudulent payment often generates both a fraud report and a dispute, so it can effectively count twice.
  • Winning a dispute doesn't remove it. Even if you prove the customer received the order, the dispute still counts in your ratio.

What changed in April 2026

On 1 April 2026, Visa lowered the "Excessive" limit for merchants in Europe (and the US, Canada and Asia-Pacific) from 2.2% to 1.5%.

Above that limit, Visa charges fees per fraud or dispute case (around $8 each at the Excessive level), and providers must put a plan in place to bring your ratio down.

"I'm a small shop. Does this even apply to me?"

Here's the honest answer: Visa's formal programme only kicks in once a merchant has 1,500 or more fraud and dispute cases in a month. Most small pharmacies and health shops are nowhere near that.

But it still affects you, for one important reason. Visa also measures your payment provider's entire portfolio, and the limits for providers are much stricter: 0.5% to 0.7%. If your provider is getting close to its own limit, it will tighten the rules for every merchant it serves, including small ones. That can mean:

  • Questions about your disputes, even at low volume
  • Higher reserves on your payouts
  • Requests to add fraud or dispute tools
  • In the worst case, your account being closed because you raise their overall ratio

So your own number matters, even if Visa never looks at you directly.

Why health merchants are more exposed

Pharmacies, supplement and wellness shops tend to see more disputes than average because of:

  • Subscriptions customers forget about
  • Unrecognised charges, when the name on the bank statement doesn't match the shop name
  • Delivery issues, especially with temperature-sensitive or prescription products
  • "Friendly fraud", where a real customer disputes a real purchase, sometimes out of embarrassment about what they bought

Seven practical ways to keep your ratio low

  1. Fix your billing descriptor. Make sure your shop or pharmacy name, as customers know it, appears on their statement. This one change prevents many disputes.
  2. Make refunds easy and fast. A refund doesn't count in the VAMP ratio. A dispute does. When in doubt, refund.
  3. Send reminders before subscription charges, and make cancelling a one-click job.
  4. Keep customers informed about delivery. Confirmation emails and tracking links reduce "item not received" disputes.
  5. Use 3-D Secure (the bank app confirmation step) on online payments. It shifts liability for many fraudulent payments away from you.
  6. Ask your provider about dispute alerts. Visa's own figures exclude disputes resolved early through certain pre-dispute services (such as Visa's Rapid Dispute Resolution). Your provider can tell you what's available.
  7. Watch for card testing. If you see lots of small, failed payments in a short time, fraudsters may be testing stolen cards on your checkout. Tell your provider straight away.

How to check where you stand

Ask your payment provider for three numbers each month:

  • Number of online Visa transactions
  • Number of disputes
  • Number of fraud reports

Add the last two, divide by the first, and you have a rough VAMP ratio. Aim to stay well below 1%, not just under 1.5%, because your provider's own limits are stricter than yours.

Figures are based on Visa's programme as published in 2026. Visa updates its rules periodically; check the latest VAMP fact sheet or ask your provider for current thresholds.

Frequently asked questions

Does Mastercard have something similar?

Yes, Mastercard runs its own monitoring programmes for excessive chargebacks and fraud, with different rules and thresholds. Keeping disputes low protects you under both.

If I win all my disputes, am I safe?

No. Won disputes still count in the VAMP ratio. Preventing disputes is far more valuable than winning them.

Can my provider close my account even if I'm under 1.5%?

Yes. Providers set their own limits based on their portfolio. That's why choosing a provider that understands your category, and talking to them early, matters.
