Cannabis Flower Is No Longer Reimbursed: What It Means for Your Pharmacy’s Payments

Since 30 July 2026, statutory health insurance (GKV) no longer pays for medical cannabis flower. For many patients, that's a shock. For your pharmacy, it changes something very practical: who pays you, and how.

This guide explains the change in plain terms and what you should put in place now so you don't lose patients at the checkout.

What changed on 30 July 2026

The GKV contribution stabilisation law (GKV-Beitragssatzstabilisierungsgesetz) rewrote the rule that governed cannabis reimbursement. In short:

  • Dried cannabis flower is no longer a GKV benefit. This applies even to patients whose therapy had already been approved. There is no transition period.
  • Flower can still be prescribed and dispensed, but only on a private prescription (Privatrezept). The patient pays.
  • Extracts, dronabinol and nabilon remain reimbursable, although new patients generally need a six-month trial with an approved finished medicine first.
  • Private insurance isn't directly affected. Whether a private insurer pays depends on the patient's policy.

Estimates suggest around 65,000 patients who used to get flower on a GKV prescription are affected. Some will switch to extracts. Many will stay on flower and pay themselves.

Why this is a payment question, not just a regulatory one

Before the reform, a big share of your cannabis revenue came through insurance settlements. You dispensed, you billed the Krankenkasse, you got paid.

Now more of it arrives as direct patient payments: at your counter, through your webshop, or via telemedicine platforms. That means:

  • More card and online payments, often for higher amounts than a typical pharmacy basket
  • More repeat purchases, as patients refill regularly
  • More scrutiny from payment providers, because cannabis sits on many providers' restricted lists

The self-pay market was already the biggest part of medical cannabis in Germany. The reform makes it even bigger. If your payment setup was built for occasional card payments at the counter, now is the time to check it can handle this.

Five things to check this month

1. Does your payment provider know you sell cannabis?

If your provider approved you as a "general pharmacy" and your cannabis sales are now growing fast, that's a risk. A sudden jump in volume or average order value is one of the most common reasons funds get held. Tell your provider now, in writing, before they notice on their own.

2. Can patients pay online?

Self-paying patients expect to order and pay the way they do everywhere else. If you only take payment on pickup, you may be losing them to online pharmacies.

3. Is your checkout clear about private prescriptions?

Make sure your webshop explains that flower is now paid privately, shows the price clearly, and asks for the prescription before payment. Surprised patients cause refunds and disputes.

4. Will the patient recognise the charge?

Your pharmacy name, as patients know it, should appear on their bank statement. Unrecognised charges are a top cause of chargebacks.

5. Do you have a backup?

If your only payment provider pauses your account, your cannabis revenue stops overnight. A second payment route protects you.

Helping patients through the change

Payments aren't only about technology. A few simple things reduce friction:

  • Explain the change at the counter and online. A short FAQ page on your website ("Why do I have to pay for my flower now?") saves a lot of phone calls.
  • Offer payment methods patients already use: cards first, plus bank transfer or instant payment for repeat patients.
  • Be upfront about prices. Patients comparing pharmacies will look at price. Hidden costs at checkout lead to abandoned orders or disputes.
  • Point patients to their doctor if they want to know whether extracts could be an option for them. Some may be able to stay within GKV reimbursement.

What to watch next

The reimbursement change isn't the only one in play. A separate amendment to the Medical Cannabis Act (MedCanG) could restrict telemedicine first prescriptions and ban mail-order of flower. It hasn't passed yet. If it does, even more patients will pay at your counter or through your delivery service, rather than via online shipping. A payment setup that handles in-store, online and pickup payments together will serve you in both scenarios.

This article is general information and not legal advice. Reimbursement rules and the MedCanG amendment are still developing; check the latest guidance from your Apothekerkammer or KV.

Frequently asked questions

Do patients with an existing GKV approval still get flower reimbursed?

No. The change applies to existing approvals too. Flower is now only available on a private prescription.

Are extracts still covered?

Yes, extracts, dronabinol and nabilon remain reimbursable, with a six-month trial with an approved finished medicine generally required for new patients.

Will my payment provider treat me differently now?

They might, if your cannabis sales grow noticeably. Talk to them before your volumes change, not after.

Can I take payments through a telemedicine platform instead?

Some platforms handle payment. Make sure it's clear who the seller is on the patient's statement and who handles refunds, and that your own provider knows how it works.
