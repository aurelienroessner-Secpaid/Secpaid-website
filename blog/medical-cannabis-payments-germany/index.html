Medical cannabis payments in Germany

Also available in Deutsch

Medical cannabis payments in Germany run on ordinary card, wallet and invoice rails, but only some payment providers will underwrite them. A telemedicine platform, pharmacy or marketplace that sells prescription cannabis needs a provider that understands the German rules.

Germany moved medical cannabis out of the narcotics act on 1 April 2024. Imports of medical cannabis flower then rose to about 201 tonnes in 2025, up from about 71 tonnes in 2024, according to BfArM figures reported by Krautinvest. The federal government attributes the gap mainly to self-paying patients who use telemedicine platforms.

The legal position below is as of 29 September 2026.

Where the law stands in September 2026

One change to medical cannabis rules has passed and one is still a draft.

The MedCanG amendment (Drucksache 21/3061) is the draft. The cabinet approved it on 8 October 2025, the Bundestag held its first reading on 18 December 2025, and the health committee heard experts on 14 January 2026. As of mid-September 2026 it had not passed the Bundestag, according to Medicanova. Check the Bundestag procedure page before you act on this.

As drafted, a first prescription of cannabis flower would need personal contact between doctor and patient. Follow-up prescriptions could use telemedicine, with one in-person consultation per four quarters. Pharmacy mail-order of medical cannabis would end, while the pharmacy courier service (Botendienst) stays. The health ministry FAQ sets out these points.

The coalition has not agreed. The SPD parliamentary group supports video consultations and opposes the blanket shipping ban, as LTO reported. The pharmacists’ chamber ABDA backs the ban.

The measure that has passed is the GKV-Beitragssatzstabilisierungsgesetz. It removes dried cannabis flower from statutory health insurance cover, and cannabis therapy under the GKV now starts with a six-month trial of an approved finished product. According to KV Rheinland-Pfalz, it took effect on 30 July 2026. Dronabinol, nabilon and standardised extracts stay reimbursable. Patients who lose cover become self-payers, so more volume moves to direct online payments.

One more point matters for payments. Since 1 April 2024, medical cannabis has been outside the narcotics act, BfArM says. A prescription is now a normal prescription-only drug, and the consumer rules of the KCanG do not apply to it.

How the money moves

A single order usually produces two or three payments: the doctor’s consultation fee, the pharmacy’s price for the medicine, and sometimes a delivery or platform fee.

Operators describe different set-ups on their own sites. Bloomwell says the patient fills in a questionnaire, a doctor issues an e-prescription, and the medicine arrives by DHL Express or is collected at one of 30-plus partner pharmacies. Its listed consultation fees are 25.22 EUR for a first consultation (currently waived) and 28.25 EUR for a follow-up, calculated under the GoÄ.

Dr. Ansay runs a marketplace with more than 450 partner pharmacies and consultations from 14.20 EUR. Its DrAnsay Pay option takes payment at checkout instead of through a later pharmacy invoice.

The entity that collects the money differs from one model to the next. In some, the pharmacy invoices the patient. In others, the platform collects at checkout and passes funds on. That choice decides who signs the payment agreement and how a provider assesses the risk, so settle it before you apply.

PayPal is under pressure in this segment. MedCanOneStop dropped PayPal on 14 May 2025 and wrote that PayPal is increasingly blocking medical cannabis providers. That is a merchant’s account of its own experience, not a PayPal statement.

Wero went live for online shops in Germany on 17 November 2025. Whether a given provider enables it for prescription products is a question to put to that provider.

Where mainstream payment providers draw the line

Three large providers publish policies that touch this vertical, and none of them says plainly that German prescription cannabis is welcome.

None of these pages says whether the provider treats German prescription cannabis as cannabis or as a prescription drug. Ask, and get the answer in writing before you build a checkout around one provider.

In a restricted category, underwriting reviews your business, your licences and your website before it decides.

German rules that reach your checkout

A payment provider’s compliance team reads your website, so advertising and pricing law matters as much as the payment flow.

On 26 March 2026 the Federal Court of Justice ruled in case I ZR 74/25 that public advertising listing the conditions treatable with cannabis therapy breaches section 10(1) of the Heilmittelwerbegesetz (HWG). The court’s press release has the details. Product pages, landing pages and checkout copy should not list conditions.

Pharmacy prices for prescription medicines follow the Arzneimittelpreisverordnung (AMPreisV). Apotheke Adhoc described the private-prescription markups in 2024, and figures may have changed since. Ask your lawyer before you offer discounts or bundles at checkout.

Payment records can also reveal health information. Whether a cannabis purchase record counts as health data under Article 9 of the GDPR is a legal question this guide does not answer. Put it to your data protection officer before you choose where to store transaction data.

What to ask a payment provider

Six questions separate a provider that can handle this vertical from one that will close your account in a few months:

  • Which entity is the merchant of record in your model, and does the contract name that entity?
  • Does the provider treat German prescription cannabis as permitted, restricted or prohibited? Ask for the answer in writing.
  • Which documents does underwriting need, such as licences and pharmacy partnership agreements?
  • What are the reserve terms and the payout schedule?
  • Which methods can you offer: cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Klarna, bank transfer, Wero?
  • What happens to your account if the MedCanG amendment changes how you prescribe or deliver?

Frequently asked questions

Is medical cannabis a high-risk category for payments in Germany?

Many providers restrict it. Adyen lists cannabis as restricted (Canada only) and prescription medicine as restricted, and Stripe prohibits “cannabis products” while reviewing online pharmacies and telemedicine. How a provider classifies your business depends on that provider.

Does the MedCanG amendment change how payments work?

Not directly, because the draft targets prescribing and delivery. Limits on telemedicine or shipping would change order flows, and a provider will ask how your model adapts.

Can I accept PayPal for medical cannabis?

PayPal requires approval for prescription products. One operator, MedCanOneStop, dropped PayPal in May 2025 after reporting blocks from PayPal.

Are payments for medical cannabis health data?

This guide cannot say. It is a question for your data protection officer or a lawyer.
