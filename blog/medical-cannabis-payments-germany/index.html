Medical Cannabis Payments in Germany: A Practical Guide for Pharmacies

Also available in Deutsch

If you run a pharmacy that dispenses medical cannabis, you've probably noticed two things. Demand has grown fast. And getting paid online is harder than it should be.

This guide is for pharmacists who also look after their own webshop. No jargon, just what you need to know to take payments reliably and keep your account open.

Why this matters more than ever in 2026

Germany is now Europe's biggest medical cannabis market. Since April 2024, medical cannabis is no longer classed as a narcotic under the BtMG, which made prescribing and dispensing much simpler. Patient numbers, telemedicine platforms and online pharmacies have grown quickly as a result.

Two things changed the payment picture this year:

Most patients now pay for themselves. The private, self-pay market already made up around three quarters of medical cannabis sales. Since 30 July 2026, statutory health insurance (GKV) no longer reimburses dried cannabis flower. Doctors can still prescribe flower, but patients who used to rely on their Krankenkasse now pay out of pocket. For your pharmacy, that means more card and online payments, and fewer insurance settlements.

The law may still change. A draft amendment to the Medical Cannabis Act (MedCanG) has been in the Bundestag since late 2025. If it passes as drafted, first prescriptions would need an in-person doctor visit, and pharmacies would no longer be allowed to ship cannabis flower by post. As of September 2026 it has not been passed, the mail-order ban does not apply, and it is unlikely to take effect before the end of 2026. Keep an eye on it, because it would change how many of your patients order and pay.

Why banks and payment providers treat cannabis as "high-risk"

Your pharmacy is fully legal and licensed. So why do some providers say no, or close accounts after a few months?

  • Cannabis is on many providers' restricted lists by default, regardless of whether it's medical or legal where you are. Automated systems often flag the word "cannabis" before a human looks at your licence.
  • Card schemes and banks look at the whole chain. They want to know that every order has a valid prescription, that you only ship to eligible patients, and that the product is what you say it is.
  • Disputes can be higher. A patient who doesn't recognise a charge on their statement, or whose order arrives late, may raise a chargeback instead of calling you.

None of this means you can't take card payments. It means you need a provider that understands pharmacies and has actually approved cannabis before.

What a payment provider will ask you for

Having these ready speeds up approval a lot:

  1. Your pharmacy operating licence (Betriebserlaubnis) and, if you ship, your mail-order permit (Versandhandelserlaubnis).
  2. How you check prescriptions: paper, e-prescription, telemedicine partners. Be ready to explain your process in two or three sentences.
  3. Which telemedicine platforms you work with, if any, and who takes the payment: you or the platform. The business that appears as the seller on the card statement carries the payment risk, so this needs to be clear.
  4. Your website, with clear prices, terms, a returns and cancellation policy, a full Impressum and contact details. Make sure patients can't order prescription products without a prescription step.
  5. Recent bank statements and processing history, if you've taken card payments before.
  6. Expected monthly volume and average order value. Estimates are fine. Just be realistic; a sudden jump far above what you declared is one of the most common reasons funds get held.

Payment methods that make sense for cannabis patients

Most patients will want to pay by card, so that's your priority. Beyond cards:

  • Bank transfer and instant payments are simple and can't be charged back like cards can. They work well for repeat patients.
  • SEPA Direct Debit is convenient, but remember customers can ask their bank to reverse a direct debit for up to eight weeks, no questions asked.
  • Wallets and buy-now-pay-later (PayPal, Klarna and similar) each have their own rules on medicines and cannabis. Check before you add them, and don't assume that because one method works, the others will too.

Five habits that keep your account healthy

  1. Use a recognisable billing descriptor. Your pharmacy name should appear on the patient's statement, not a holding company name they've never heard of. This alone prevents a lot of "I don't recognise this" disputes.
  2. Send a clear order confirmation and tracking. Late or unclear deliveries are a common dispute trigger.
  3. Answer refund requests quickly. A refund you issue costs you far less than a chargeback the bank forces.
  4. Tell your provider before big changes. New telemedicine partner, a big marketing push, a new product range: a two-line email beforehand avoids a surprise freeze afterwards.
  5. Have a backup. Relying on a single provider is risky in any high-risk category. If your only account is paused, your shop stops. A second route for payments keeps you trading.

This article is for general information and isn't legal advice. Rules around medical cannabis are changing; check the current status of the MedCanG amendment before making decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Stripe for medical cannabis?

Generally no. Stripe says payments for prescription medication need to happen outside its platform, and it currently restricts any products containing CBD or THC.

Do I need a special "high-risk" merchant account?

You need a provider that is willing to approve medical cannabis and knows how to underwrite a pharmacy. Whether it's labelled "high-risk" matters less than whether they understand your business.

What happens to my payments if the mail-order ban passes?

If flower can no longer be shipped, more patients will pick up in store or use your delivery service (Botendienst). You'll want a provider that handles online payment with in-store pickup, and in-person card payments, not just webshop checkout.

Can the telemedicine platform take payment on my behalf?

Some models work this way, but the setup needs to be clear to your payment provider and to the patient. Check who is the seller on the statement and who handles refunds before you sign anything.
