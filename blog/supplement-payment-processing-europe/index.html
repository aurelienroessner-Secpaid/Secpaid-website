Nutraceutical and supplement payment processing in Europe

Supplement merchants in Europe can accept cards, but they need a payment provider that understands EU health-claims rules and prices dispute risk realistically. Supplement payment processing in Europe depends on three things: what your product pages claim, how many customers dispute a charge, and which local payment methods your buyers expect.

The position below is as of 29 September 2026.

It is written for merchants selling vitamins, minerals, botanicals, sports nutrition and similar products online from Germany. EU rules apply in every member state, and the German points are marked. CBD products sit under stricter rules, and the guide flags where.

Why acquirers treat supplements as higher risk

Acquirers worry about disputes from subscriptions and about regulatory exposure from health claims.

Mollie says chargebacks are more prevalent in supplements, without giving a figure. Subscription billing adds to that. Stripe’s restricted businesses list prohibits negative option marketing and reduced-price trials with unclear or hidden pricing, the practices behind many disputes.

Providers also sort merchants by category code. Checkout.com’s regulated platform policy places nutraceuticals and dietary supplements under MCC 5499 with pre-approval, and treats free-trial and negative-option subscriptions under MCC 5968 as restricted. That policy is written for payment facilitators, so a direct merchant may see different terms.

Contract terms are the other place risk shows. Ask each provider what reserve it applies, and for how long, before you sign.

EU rules that shape underwriting

A payment provider’s compliance team reads your product pages against EU rules on health claims and novel foods.

Under Regulation (EC) 1924/2006, only health claims approved in the Union Register are allowed, and non-approved health claims have been banned since 14 December 2012, according to Germany’s agriculture ministry. An Advocate General opinion in CJEU case C-721/24, delivered on 29 January 2026, defines a health claim by how consumers perceive it and suggests that dosage statements and distance-selling websites can fall within the rules, as this law firm summary explains. It is not a final ruling, so check whether the court has decided since.

CBD is a separate case. As of February 2026, no CBD ingredient had been authorised as a novel food in the EU, according to Complife, and EFSA set a provisional safe intake of 0.0275 mg per kg of body weight per day. Germany’s BVL says such products are not marketable without authorisation.

In September 2026 EFSA gave a positive opinion on an oil-based supplement with synthetic CBD of at least 98% purity, for adults over 25 under narrow conditions. The Commission and Member States still have to decide on authorisation.

For the underlying texts, read Directive 2002/46/EC on food supplements and Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 on novel foods on EUR-Lex. A lawyer familiar with your target markets should review your claims before you apply.

How the card schemes monitor disputes

Both Visa and Mastercard flag merchants whose disputes climb, and Visa lowered its EU merchant threshold to 1.5% on 1 April 2026.

Several large providers accept some supplements after review. They differ on CBD and on how much documentation they want.

See our comparison of Mollie, Adyen and Shopify Payments for the full wording.

For an application in Germany, expect requests like those of PAYONE: a company self-disclosure, a commercial register excerpt not older than six months, ID copies of the authorised signatories, a bank confirmation with IBAN and company name, and proof of PCI compliance. Its risk check also reads your terms and conditions (AGB), legal notice (Impressum) and privacy notice. PAYONE puts onboarding at about nine business days when documents are complete, which is one provider’s estimate.

Supplements have their own German step. Under section 5 of the NemV, a supplement must be notified to the BVL before its first sale, and the authorities do not examine or approve it beforehand. Add product labels and ingredient lists to your application.

Our suggestion, not a sourced requirement: add evidence that each ingredient is lawful in Germany and the substantiation behind every health claim on your site.

Checklist and frequently asked questions

Before you apply, confirm these points:

  • Every health claim on your site is on the EU’s approved list or removed.
  • Your supplement is notified to the BVL under section 5 NemV before its first sale in Germany.
  • Each ingredient is lawful to sell in Germany, and CBD products have been checked against novel food rules.
  • Trial length, renewal price and cancellation steps sit next to the pay button.
  • Your billing descriptor shows your trading name.
  • You have estimated your dispute ratio and know where it sits against the 1.5% Visa level.
  • You know which payment methods your German buyers expect.

Frequently asked questions

Are supplements high-risk for payments in Europe?

Many providers review them individually. Mollie says chargebacks are more prevalent in supplements, Adyen lists them as restricted, and Stripe prohibits unsafe or harmfully claimed nutraceuticals. Whether you are treated as high-risk depends on the provider and on your dispute record.

Can I sell CBD supplements in the EU with a card processor?

Germany’s BVL says CBD foods are not marketable without novel food authorisation, and Klarna bans ingestible CBD across the EU. EFSA’s September 2026 opinion on a synthetic CBD supplement is not yet an authorisation.

How long does approval take?

PAYONE, a German provider, says about nine business days when all documents arrive complete. That is one provider’s estimate, and it grows when documents are missing. If you want to compare options for a supplement business, talk to SecPaid.
