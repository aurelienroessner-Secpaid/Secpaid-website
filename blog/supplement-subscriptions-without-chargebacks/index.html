Supplement Subscriptions Without Chargebacks

Subscriptions are the best thing that can happen to a supplement shop. Predictable revenue, loyal customers, less marketing spend.

They're also the biggest source of chargebacks in the category. A customer forgets they signed up, sees a charge, doesn't recognise it, and calls their bank. You lose the sale, pay a fee, and your dispute rate goes up.

The good news: most subscription disputes are preventable. Here's how.

Why subscriptions cause so many disputes

Almost none of these disputes come from criminals. They come from real customers who:

  • Forgot they subscribed, especially after a discount or trial
  • Don't recognise the name on their bank statement
  • Tried to cancel but couldn't, or didn't know how
  • Changed their mind and found disputing easier than contacting you
  • Feel embarrassed about what they bought, for example weight-management or intimate health products

When a real customer disputes a real purchase, it's often called "friendly fraud". It isn't friendly for you, but it's usually not malicious either. It's a communication problem, which means you can fix most of it.

Why this matters more in 2026

Card schemes have become stricter about disputes. Visa lowered its dispute limit for European merchants to 1.5% in April 2026, and payment providers have even tighter limits across their whole portfolio. They're passing that pressure down to merchants. A supplement shop with a rising dispute rate is exactly what they're watching for. (We explain this in our guide to Visa's VAMP rules.)

Seven fixes that prevent most disputes

1. Say it plainly before payment

Right next to the pay button:

"You'll be charged €29.90 now and every 30 days until you cancel. Cancel anytime in your account."

No small print. No pre-ticked boxes.

2. Make the button say what it does

In Germany, the order button must make clear the customer is committing to pay, for example "zahlungspflichtig bestellen". For subscriptions, "Subscribe and pay €29.90/month" is even clearer.

3. Send a confirmation that's easy to find later

Subject line: "Your [shop name] subscription: €29.90 every 30 days." Include the next charge date and a direct cancel link.

4. Remind before charging

Send an email a few days before each charge, and always before the first full-price charge after a discount or trial. Card schemes have specific rules for free trials, including reminders, so ask your payment provider what applies to you.

5. Make cancelling easy

In Germany, online subscription contracts must offer a cancellation button (Kündigungsbutton) on the website. Beyond the legal minimum: one click in the customer account, confirmed by email. Every customer who can't cancel easily is a potential chargeback.

6. Use a billing descriptor people recognise

The name on the bank statement should be your shop name as customers know it, ideally with a short website or phone number. "SP*VITALSHOP.DE" beats "XYZ HOLDING GMBH" every time.

7. Refund faster than they can dispute

If a customer emails to complain about a charge, refund quickly. A refund costs you the sale. A dispute costs you the sale, a fee, and a mark against your account.

Pay attention to your payment methods

  • Cards are the standard for subscriptions and work everywhere.
  • SEPA Direct Debit is popular in Germany, but customers can ask their bank to reverse a direct debit for up to eight weeks with no reason needed. The same communication habits above apply.
  • Update expired cards before they fail. Many providers offer automatic card updates. Failed payments followed by a sudden retry can confuse customers.

Handling "sensitive" products

For weight-management, intimate health or similar products, customers may dispute a charge rather than let a partner see it on a statement. You can reduce this by:

  • Using a neutral but recognisable descriptor: your shop name, not the product name
  • Shipping in discreet packaging, and saying so on the product page
  • Making customer service easy to reach, so embarrassment doesn't turn into a bank call

Track three numbers every month

  1. Disputes ÷ transactions: aim well below 1%
  2. Refunds ÷ transactions: a rising refund rate often comes before rising disputes
  3. Cancellation requests you couldn't resolve: a warning sign

If any of these starts climbing, look at your last change: new product, new offer, new checkout wording. The cause is usually there.

This article is general information and not legal advice. Consumer law requirements for subscriptions vary by country.

Frequently asked questions

Are free trials still worth it?

They can work, but they're the highest-risk subscription model. If you use them, make the full price and the date of the first charge unmissable, and send reminders.

Should I fight every chargeback?

Fight the ones you can clearly win, with delivery proof and a clear subscription agreement. But prevention is worth far more: even won disputes can still count against you with card schemes.

My provider says my dispute rate is too high. What now?

Act quickly. Fix your descriptor, checkout wording and cancellation flow, tell your provider what you've changed, and ask for time to show the improvement.
