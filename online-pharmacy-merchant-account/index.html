Why online pharmacy payments are high-risk

Selling regulated medicine online, card-not-present, is one of the categories acquiring banks scrutinise most. The transaction combines several risks at once: potential counterfeit-drug liability, prescription validation, controlled-substance rules that vary by country, and cross-border shipping that crosses regulatory lines.

Online pharmacies also see high chargeback and friendly-fraud rates, and they handle patient data, which brings GDPR into scope. European data protection authorities issued around 22.8 million euros in healthcare-sector fines in 2024 alone, covering pharmacies, hospitals, and medical suppliers. A processor that stores card data loosely or hosts outside the EU adds to that exposure.

The chargeback drivers are specific too. Delivery disputes, "item not as described" claims on generic versus branded medication, and customers who forget a recurring refill order all land as chargebacks, and a regulator takedown of a product mid-sale can trigger several at once. An acquirer that has not underwritten a pharmacy before treats each of these as a reason to decline rather than a manageable risk.

Mainstream processors decline online pharmacies as a rule. The specialists that approve them often do so through a single acquirer, which means one policy change or one bad chargeback month can end your ability to take card payments.

What prescription-aware underwriting means in practice

Your application is reviewed against how a pharmacy actually operates. That means checking your dispensing licence and the authorisations for each country you ship to, understanding whether prescriptions are validated in-house or through a partner prescriber, and confirming how you handle refill scheduling and cancellations. The result is a merchant account configured for your catalogue and markets, not a generic e-commerce account that gets frozen the first time an acquirer notices you sell medicine.

If your model changes, adding a country, a product category, or a prescriber partner, your account manager updates the configuration and, where needed, brings another acquirer into the routing rules. You do not restart underwriting from zero each time you grow.
